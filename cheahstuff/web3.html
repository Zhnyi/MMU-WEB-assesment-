Computer hardware in action

[Where is computer hardware actually used?]

The previous page explained what each hardware component is and what it does. This page shows where that hardware is actually used. The same components appear almost everywhere, but what each field needs from them is different. This webpage will explain how the computer hardware is applied or relevant.

Business

Priority: matching specifications to each department's workload. All departments work in different software, meaning every department has different hardware specifications.

Education

Priority: affordable machines that many students can share. There are a huge amount of total students per school, so we need affordable but functioning hardware to cover for the right amount of hardware that can accomodate each schools.

Healthcare

Priority: display clarity and hardware that does not fail. Failure risks health issues and loss of life, so hardware in healthcare must be precise and efficient, which will come at high costs.

Daily life

Priority: portability and convenience to make day-to-day life easier.

[Business]

Businesses rely on computer hardware daily, from recording sales to producing marketing content. Because different departments do different work, the hardware given to each one is usually different as well. Choosing correctly keeps costs down, while still giving every team enough performance to work efficiently, in other words, businesses try to achieve the best ROI (Return on Investment) when it comes to purchasing and using hardware.

Examples in business

  • Retail shops use point-of-sale systems (otherwise known as POS kiosk), barcode scanners, and receipt printers so that sales can be recorded effectively.
  • Finance and administration staff use desktop computers with dual monitors and a scanner, so invoices and receipts can be digitally recorded and cross-checked.
  • Design teams use systems with a dedicated graphics card, because image and video editing needs far more graphics processing than documents or email.
  • Offices share one multifunction peripheral for printing, scanning, and copying instead of buying a separate machine for each of those three tasks.

No two departments necessarily receive the same computer. A design workstation costs more than an administration desktop, but giving that same workstation to every employee would be a waste, and giving the administration desktop to the design team would slow their work down.

[Education]

Schools and universities need hardware that can serve a large number of students at a reasonable cost. Lab machines are usually mid-range rather than high performance, because most coursework involves documents, research, and basic programming rather than heavy rendering. Long-lastingness takes priority since the same computer may be used by dozens of different students every week.

Examples in education

  • Computer labs use standard desktop systems with integrated graphics, which are enough for word processing, programming practice, and web browsing.
  • Lecture halls use a data projector connected to the lecturer's laptop so that slides can be shown to an entire class at once with a clear view.
  • Students use webcams and microphones for online classes to see each other and speak with each other.

Education also depends on hardware that students own themselves. A student laptop has to handle note taking, assignment writing, and online classes on battery power, which makes portability and battery life more important than maximum processing speed, especially during longer classes with no outlets nearby to charge it.

[Healthcare]

In healthcare, hardware choices can affect the quality of patient care, so reliability and display precision are required. Hospital systems also tend to run continuously instead of being shut down at the end of a working day, which makes the cooling system and a correctly matched power supply more important than they would be in a typical office.

Examples in healthcare

  • Radiology departments use high-resolution monitors to view scans such as X-rays, where image clarity directly supports an accurate diagnosis.
  • Reception and records staff use desktop computers with scanners to digitise patient forms, referral letters, and consent documents.
  • Servers holding patient records use multiple hard disks so that records are not lost if a single drive fails.

This is the area where the limitations discussed on the previous page matter most. Physical wear is a normal limitation of hardware, but in a hospital a hardware failure may cost a life, so the hardware is chosen and maintained with that consequence in mind.

[Daily life]

For day-today life, most people use computer hardware without ever thinking about the individual components inside it. Portability and convenience usually matter more than raw performance here, which is why laptops and phones have become the most common personal devices rather than desktop system units.

Examples in daily life

  • A laptop combines a keyboard, pointing device, display, webcam, microphone, and speakers into a single portable unit, replacing what used to be six separate devices on a desk.
  • People working from home often connect an external monitor, keyboard, and mouse to a laptop, creating a more comfortable setup without buying a second computer.
  • Gaming systems depend heavily on a dedicated graphics card, and on a cooling system able to handle long periods of high temperature.
